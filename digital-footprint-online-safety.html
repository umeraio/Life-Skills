🛡️ Secure your essentials first

Highest priority

You do not need advanced technical skills. Start with the accounts that can unlock other accounts, hold your money, prove your identity or receive security codes.

Start with these five: your main email, government-service logins, banking apps and websites, your password manager, and your mobile phone. If a criminal controls one of these, they may be able to reset other accounts.

What is 2FA (two-factor authentication)?

2FA means proving it is really you in two different ways. First, you enter something you know, such as a password. Then you confirm with something you have, such as an authenticator-app code, your phone prompt or a security key.

PASSWORD ONLY
🔓 A stolen password may be enough to log in

If a password is guessed, reused after a data breach or tricked out of someone, there is no second check.

PASSWORD + 2FA
🔐 The attacker also needs your second proof

This makes account takeover much harder. An authenticator app is usually safer than receiving codes by text message.

How to set up 2FA with Google Authenticator
  1. Install Google Authenticator only from the official Apple App Store or Google Play Store.
  2. On the account you want to protect, open Security, Two-step verification or Authenticator app.
  3. Choose to add an authenticator app. The website will show a QR code.
  4. Open Google Authenticator, press +, then choose Scan a QR code.
  5. Scan the code. The app creates a short code that changes regularly.
  6. Type the current code into the website to confirm setup.
  7. Save the account’s recovery codes somewhere safe, separate from your phone. Do not share them.

When signing in later: enter your password, then open Google Authenticator and enter the current code shown for that account.

Never share security codes: a real bank, government service, email provider or password manager should not ask you to read out, forward or type a one-time code into an unexpected call, message or website.

Protect account recovery methods

Your recovery email address, recovery phone number and recovery codes can be used to regain access to an account. Keep these details correct and private.

  • Review recovery email addresses and phone numbers on important accounts at least twice a year.
  • Remove old phone numbers, old email addresses and devices you no longer own.
  • Store 2FA recovery codes in a secure place, not in a public note or ordinary photo gallery.
  • Check account-security pages for unfamiliar sign-ins. Sign out of devices you do not recognise.
  • If you suspect an account was accessed, change its password immediately and review recovery details.

What are passkeys?

A passkey lets you sign in using your phone or computer’s screen lock, such as your fingerprint, face unlock or PIN. Instead of typing a password, your device proves it is you. Passkeys are designed to be resistant to fake login pages because they are linked to the real website or app.

  1. Sign in to an important account using its official app or website.
  2. Open Security, Sign-in options or Passkeys.
  3. Choose Create passkey or Add passkey.
  4. Confirm with your phone’s fingerprint, face unlock or PIN.
  5. Next time, choose Sign in with a passkey and approve it on your phone.

Note: the exact wording differs by provider, and not every service offers passkeys yet. Use them wherever offered on important accounts, alongside the provider’s recovery options.

Password management: make remembering easier, not weaker

Do not rely only on a web browser to hold every password. A reputable dedicated password manager such as Bitwarden can generate and store long, unique passwords in an encrypted vault. A free Bitwarden account can be suitable for many people who struggle to remember multiple passwords.

  • Create one long, memorable master password for the password manager. Never reuse it anywhere else.
  • Enable 2FA on the Bitwarden account itself. Your password manager is one of your most sensitive accounts.
  • Let the password manager create a different password for each site.
  • Never tell anyone your master password, recovery phrase or password-manager codes.

🍪 Cookies: what they are and how to delete them

Browser basics

A cookie is a small piece of information that a website saves in your browser. It can help a site remember you, but it can also be used to track browsing activity for advertising or analytics.

🛒

Useful cookies

They remember items in a shopping basket, language choice or that you are signed in during a visit.

📊

Analytics cookies

They help a site understand how people use its pages so it can improve them.

🎯

Advertising cookies

They can help build a picture of interests and show more targeted adverts across sites.

Are cookies safe? Cookies are not normally viruses and cannot physically damage your computer. However, they can affect privacy, and a stolen login cookie can sometimes be misused. It is sensible to clear browsing data occasionally and accept only necessary cookies when you have a choice.

Before you clear cookies

Clearing cookies may sign you out of websites and remove saved basket items or preferences. Make sure you know your important passwords or have access to your password manager first.

Delete cookies and browsing data in Google Chrome
  1. Open Chrome and select the three dots in the top-right corner.
  2. Choose Delete browsing data (or Settings → Privacy and security → Delete browsing data).
  3. Select a time range, such as All time.
  4. Tick Cookies and other site data. You may also choose cached images and files.
  5. Choose Delete data.
Delete cookies and browsing data in Microsoft Edge
  1. Open Edge and select the three dots in the top-right corner.
  2. Choose Settings → Privacy, search and services.
  3. Under “Clear browsing data”, choose Choose what to clear.
  4. Select a time range and tick Cookies and other site data.
  5. Select Clear now.
Delete cookies and browsing data in Firefox
  1. Open Firefox and select the menu button.
  2. Choose Settings → Privacy & Security.
  3. Under Cookies and Site Data, select Clear Data.
  4. Choose the data you want to remove, then select Clear.
Extra privacy tip: use your browser’s private or incognito mode when needed. It mainly prevents local history and cookies being kept on that device after the private window closes. It does not make you invisible to websites, your network or your internet provider.

Keep Windows Security enabled

Windows Security includes built-in protection against viruses and other threats. Keep real-time protection and automatic updates enabled unless a trusted IT professional has a specific reason to change them. If you use another reputable security product, avoid running several full antivirus products at the same time because they can conflict.

USEFUL AND LOW RISK
✅ Built-in storage cleanup and uninstalling unused apps

These are usually enough for normal maintenance. Review what is selected before deleting files.

BE CAREFUL
⚠️ “PC cleaner” pop-ups and registry cleaners

Many promise dramatic speed improvements but can be unnecessary or install extra unwanted software. Tools such as CCleaner are mainly useful for removing temporary files; they are not a replacement for updates, backups or security protection. Download only from the official source and avoid aggressive registry cleaning.

Browser extensions: less is safer

Extensions can read or change what you see in your browser. Install only extensions you genuinely need, from the official browser store, made by a recognisable publisher. Review and remove old extensions regularly. Never install an extension because a pop-up claims it will “fix” a problem.

✓Keep the phone operating system and apps updated.
✓Turn on “Find My Device” or the equivalent recovery feature.
✓Install apps only from official app stores.
✓Protect your phone carefully: it may approve passkeys and 2FA logins.

👣 What is a digital footprint?

Start here

A digital footprint is the trail of information connected to you when you use the internet. It includes what you choose to share and information collected in the background.

✍️

Active footprint

Information you deliberately create or share: a post, comment, online review, CV, photo, email or form.

👀

Passive footprint

Information collected while you browse: cookies, device type, approximate location, pages visited and adverts you click.

🧩

Digital imprint

Another name for your online trace. Small pieces of data can build a detailed picture when combined.

Simple example: one ordinary afternoon online

1SearchYou look up trainers.
→
2BrowseA website stores a cookie.
→
3ClickYou open a social-media advert.
→
4ShareYou post a photo wearing them.

The result: each action can add to your footprint. This does not mean every action is bad. It means it is worth being deliberate.

RISKY SHARE
⚠️ “Away for two weeks. Our house is empty!”

This may reveal that your home is unoccupied and show when you will return.

SAFER SHARE
✅ Post holiday photos after you return

You can still enjoy sharing memories without advertising your location in real time.

Quick footprint check

✓Search your name occasionally and see what is public.
✓Think before posting a photo, location or personal detail.
✓Remove accounts you no longer use.
✓Review privacy settings at least twice a year.

🗣️ Privacy, reputation and respect

Pause before posting

A post can be copied, forwarded or screenshot. Even if you delete it later, someone else may already have saved it.

The 10-second sharing test

Before you post, comment, send or forward: “Would I be comfortable if my family, employer, school or future employer saw this?”
↓
Does it reveal private information, a live location, a password, a child’s details, or something about another person without permission?
↓
Yes or not sure? Pause. Change it, make it private, or do not share it.
No? Check the audience and share respectfully.
DO NOT POST
📄 A photo of a boarding pass, bank card or certificate

It may show a booking code, full name, date of birth, address or other details that can be misused.

BETTER OPTION
🖼️ Share a cropped photo with private details hidden

Or keep personal documents completely offline and private.

Create a positive footprint too

Your footprint can help you. A professional profile, helpful work, qualifications, respectful comments and accurate contact details can support jobs, education and business opportunities.

  • Use a professional email address for applications and work.
  • Keep LinkedIn and professional profiles accurate and up to date.
  • Ask permission before posting photographs of other people.
  • Do not forward embarrassing content about someone else.

🔐 Protect your accounts

Passwords + 2-step verification

Your email account is especially important because it can be used to reset passwords for shopping, social media and banking accounts.

🔑

Use a unique password

Each important account needs its own password. One reused password can unlock many accounts.

🧠

Use a password manager

It can create and securely remember long, different passwords for you.

📲

Turn on 2-step verification

After your password, the site asks for a second proof, such as an authenticator app code.

🤫

Never share a code

A bank, courier or genuine company will not call to ask you for a one-time security code.

WEAK

Easy to guess or reuse after a data breach.

STRONGER
✅ A long, unique passphrase

For example, make a memorable sentence with unrelated words, numbers and symbols. Do not use this exact example: create your own.

🎣 Scams and phishing

Stop • Check • Protect

Phishing is a fake message designed to make you click, pay, download something or reveal information. Scammers use email, text messages, social media, calls and messaging apps.

Messages
Royal Parcel Service
Your parcel is held. Pay £1.99 now to avoid return.
bit.ly/claim-parcel-now
Bank Security
Urgent: confirm your code to stop fraud.
Reply with the 6-digit code.

These are examples. A real-looking name does not prove a message is genuine.

Warning signs

  • Pressure: “Act now”, “final warning” or “your account will close”.
  • A request for money, password, code or bank details.
  • An unexpected link, attachment or QR code.
  • Odd spelling, generic greeting or a strange sender address.
  • An offer that sounds too good to be true.
SAFE ACTION
✅ Never use the message link to check

Instead, open the official app yourself, type the official website address manually, or use a phone number from a trusted statement or card. Do not use a number supplied by the suspicious message.

The “Stop, Check, Protect” method

  1. Stop: do not click, reply, download or send money.
  2. Check: independently contact the organisation using a trusted route.
  3. Protect: block and report the message. Tell someone if you are unsure.

How fake web addresses fool people

Hackers often use a web address that looks almost right. The important part is the real domain name: the last main name immediately before .com, .co.uk, .org or a similar ending. Do not rely on the logo, a padlock icon or the first word in the address.

Example of a genuine-looking domain patternhttps://www.paypal.com/signin
Fake example: the real domain is “secure-check.example”https://paypal.com.secure-check.example/signin
Fake example: a small spelling changehttps://www.paypaI.com/signin — this uses a capital “I” instead of a lower-case “l”

Simple rule: read the address from right to left. In the second example, the site belongs to secure-check.example, not PayPal. The examples above are shown for learning only and should not be visited.

Five steps to check a website address before you use it
  1. Pause: do not click an unexpected link. Press and hold on mobile, or hover with a mouse, to preview where it wants to send you.
  2. Read the domain: look for the main name immediately before the ending such as .com or .co.uk.
  3. Look for tricks: extra words, spelling changes, random letters, unusual endings and misleading subdomains.
  4. Use your own route: type the organisation’s known address yourself, use its official app, or find it through a trusted search result.
  5. Check, but do not depend only on a checker: use Google’s Safe Browsing Site Status checker to see whether a site has been flagged for dangerous content. A “not flagged” result does not prove a new or convincing scam is safe.
What if a caller says they are from your bank?

Do not trust the caller ID. Say you will call back. Use a different phone if possible, or wait a short time before calling the number printed on your bank card. Never move money to a “safe account” because of an unexpected call.

📸 Social media and messaging safely

Share with care

Social media can be enjoyable and useful. The important question is: who can see your information, and what could happen if it is copied or used out of context?

RiskEveryday exampleWhat to do
Live locationA public post says you are at the airport or on holiday.Share after you leave or return. Turn off location tagging unless needed.
Fake profiles“Your friend” asks for money, but their account was copied.Call or message them using a known number before sending anything.
OversharingA child’s school uniform, name and daily routine are visible in a photo.Crop images, hide names and keep family posts private.
Private groupsA message from a private group is screenshot and shared.Assume anything online can be copied.

Privacy settings: a simple routine

  1. Open the privacy or account settings in each platform.
  2. Set your audience to friends, contacts or private where appropriate.
  3. Check who can tag you and who can view tags.
  4. Review your follower and friend list. Remove people you do not know.
  5. Turn off public location sharing and unnecessary contact syncing.
Kindness is security too: do not forward humiliating photos, threats, hateful posts or rumours. Save evidence and report serious bullying, blackmail or threats to the platform and a trusted adult, employer, school or the police where appropriate.

💻 Safe browsing, downloads and devices

Keep it updated

Most security problems are made easier when devices are out of date, unlocked, or used to download unknown files.

⬆️

Install updates

Updates often fix security weaknesses. Turn on automatic updates for your phone, computer, browser and apps.

🔒

Lock your screen

Use a PIN, fingerprint or face unlock. Set your screen to lock quickly when not in use.

⬇️

Download carefully

Only use official app stores and trusted websites. Do not install “free” software from a random pop-up.

💾

Back up important files

Keep a copy of important photos and documents in a secure cloud service or separate backup drive.

PUBLIC WI-FI RISK
☕ Logging in to banking on free café Wi-Fi

Public networks can be unsafe or even fake. Avoid banking, shopping and sensitive logins on them.

SAFER CHOICE
📶 Use mobile data or wait for a trusted network

If you must use public Wi-Fi, avoid sensitive activity and turn off automatic network connection afterwards.

Pop-up warning: “Your computer is infected! Call this number now!” is often a scam. Do not call, allow remote access or install what it suggests. Close the browser, run trusted security software and ask someone you trust if unsure.

🛒 Online shopping and banking

Protect your money

Criminals often copy the appearance of known shops, banks and delivery companies. A professional-looking website is not proof that it is genuine.

Before you pay: a simple check

Did you arrive at this site from an unexpected advert, text, social-media post or link?
↓
Is the price unbelievably low, are contact details missing, or does the web address look slightly wrong?
↓
Yes or unsure? Do not pay. Search for the retailer independently and check reviews from reliable sources.
No obvious warning signs? Still use a protected payment method and keep your confirmation.

Safer payment habits

  • Type the retailer’s website address yourself rather than using an unexpected message link.
  • Check the full web address carefully. Look for spelling changes and extra words.
  • Use a credit card or trusted payment method where appropriate, rather than bank transfer to an unknown seller.
  • Turn on banking app alerts so you notice unusual spending quickly.
  • Check statements regularly and report unfamiliar payments immediately.
Never be rushed: a genuine bank will not ask you to transfer your money to a “safe account”, give your PIN, or share a one-time code. If in doubt, stop and contact the bank through its official app or number.

🚨 If something goes wrong

Act quickly, do not panic

Many people are caught by scams because the messages are convincing and urgent. The most helpful thing is to act quickly once you notice a problem.

1StopDo not send more money or information.
→
2SecureChange passwords from a safe device.
→
3ContactCall your bank or provider using official details.
→
4ReportReport the scam and keep evidence.
If this happened…Do this now
You clicked a suspicious linkClose the page. Do not enter details. Run a security scan and change any password you entered.
You gave away a password or codeChange the password immediately, sign out of other sessions and enable two-step verification. Contact the provider.
You sent money to a scammerContact your bank immediately using the number on your card or official app. Explain exactly what happened.
Your social account was hackedUse the platform’s official recovery process, change email password, warn contacts and remove unknown logins.
You are being bullied, threatened or blackmailedDo not engage. Save evidence, block/report, and tell a trusted person. Seek urgent help if there is immediate danger.
Important: Being scammed is not something to be ashamed of. Scammers are trained to create urgency, fear and trust. Tell someone early so you can get help faster.

✅ Your everyday online safety checklist

Take action today

You do not need to do everything at once. Start with your email, banking and main social-media accounts.

10 habits that make a real difference

1Use a different, strong password for every important account.
2Turn on 2-step verification, especially for email and banking.
3Keep phones, apps, browsers and computers updated.
4Pause before clicking unexpected links or attachments.
5Never share one-time codes, PINs or full passwords.
6Review app permissions and social-media privacy settings.
7Do not announce live location, holidays or an empty home publicly.
8Use official websites and apps for shopping, banking and deliveries.
9Back up important files and lock your devices.
10Tell someone and report quickly if you think you have been scammed.
