Do not wait until you are locked out

Account recovery options are your emergency spare keys. If you drop your phone in water, forget a complex password, or find your account temporarily locked, recovery methods prove you are the real owner. Setting them up in advance takes five minutes and prevents permanent account loss.

The Golden Security Rule: Never share a recovery code! A real company representative will never call, text, or email asking you to read a backup recovery code aloud. Anyone who gets hold of your backup codes can bypass your password and two-factor authentication in seconds.

If an outdated phone number remains linked to your profile, the mobile phone network will eventually reassign that number to a complete stranger. If that person requests a password reset, they could take over your account without guessing your password.

1Check and update your recovery email and phone

Every major service (Google, Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, and social media) has an account security dashboard where your recovery contacts live.

Official App or Website → Profile Avatar → Account Settings → Security → Recovery Information
  1. Verify your recovery email: Look at the listed fallback email address. Ensure it is a personal account you can sign in to right now. Never use a temporary school email or work address that will be deleted when you leave.
  2. Audit your recovery mobile number: Check the numbers carefully digit by digit. If you changed network providers, got a new SIM card, or switched numbers recently, update it immediately.
  3. Delete old contact routes: If you see an old family landline, an outdated mobile number, or an ex-partner's email, delete them immediately so reset links cannot be sent there.
  4. Confirm the verification prompt: The service will send a 6-digit confirmation code to your phone or backup inbox. Type that code to confirm the new details are locked in.
  5. Follow the priority order: Update your primary email account first (e.g. your Gmail or Apple ID), followed by your password manager, banking apps, and gaming or social media profiles.

2Save emergency 2FA backup codes safely

Whenever you turn on two-factor authentication (2FA) with Google Authenticator or Microsoft Authenticator, the website generates a set of one-use backup recovery codes (usually 8 or 10 codes). These are your ultimate lifeline if your phone gets lost or stolen.

How to generate your codes

  1. Open the account security page (e.g. myaccount.google.com > Security > 2-Step Verification).
  2. Scroll down to the section named Backup codes (sometimes called Recovery codes).
  3. Click + Get backup codes or Generate codes.
  4. A list of ten 8-digit numbers will appear on your screen.
  5. Each number can only be used once. When you use a code to sign in, that code expires forever.

Where to store them safely

  1. The Paper Method (Best): Grab a real pen and paper. Write the 10 codes down clearly and keep the paper in a private drawer in your bedroom.
  2. Password Manager Vault: Save the codes inside an encrypted password manager like Bitwarden under a secure note.
  3. Never take a screenshot: Never leave a photo of your recovery codes in your unencrypted camera roll or phone gallery where apps might read it.
  4. Never email them to yourself: If someone breaks into your email, they will find the codes. Keep them offline.
Single Point of Failure Warning: Never store your recovery codes exclusively on the exact same phone that holds your authenticator app! If that phone falls into the sea or gets stolen, you lose both your authenticator app and your backup codes together.

3Prepare for a new phone or a lost device

Switching phones or dealing with a lost mobile is the number one reason people get locked out of their accounts. Follow these steps:

A. Before you trade in or sell your old phone

  1. Keep both phones side-by-side: Do not wipe your old phone until your new phone is completely set up and verified.
  2. Enable Cloud Sync in Authenticator: Open Google Authenticator on your old phone and ensure your main Gmail account is logged in with the green cloud icon active.
  3. Download Authenticator on the new phone: Sign into that exact same Google account; all your 6-digit codes will transfer automatically.
  4. Test a normal login: Open a laptop, try logging in to an account, and confirm that the new phone produces the working code.
  5. Factory reset the old phone: Once verified, go to Settings and wipe the old phone completely before handing it over.

B. If your phone is already lost or stolen

  1. Remotely lock the phone: Open icloud.com/find (Apple) or android.com/find (Google) from a computer and click Lost Mode or Secure Device.
  2. Call your mobile provider: Ask them to freeze your SIM card and block the IMEI number so no one can intercept incoming SMS codes.
  3. Use your emergency backup codes: On your laptop, sign in to your main email using your password and one of your written 8-digit backup codes.
  4. Alert your bank: If mobile banking apps were on the lost device, ring your bank fraud team immediately to suspend mobile access.

4Audit linked devices and security logs

Regular maintenance ensures no forgotten computers or former roommates retain access to your profiles:

Account Profile → Security → Your Devices / Active Sessions → Manage All Devices
  1. Inspect the device list: Check the names, browser types, and approximate geographic locations of every machine listed.
  2. Remove old hardware: If you see a phone you traded in last year, a friend's tablet, or an old school computer, click on it and select Sign Out or Remove from Account.
  3. Regenerate backup codes if compromised: If you ever lost a notebook page containing your codes, or typed one into a suspicious screen, open your security dashboard and click Get new codes. This instantly destroys all old backup numbers.
  4. Change your password: If you see an unrecognized device from another city or country, click Sign Out and change your password immediately.

5What to do if you are currently locked out

If you cannot log in, follow this step-by-step recovery process calmly:

  1. Go directly to the official recovery page: Type the official address yourself (e.g. accounts.google.com/signin/recovery or iforgot.apple.com). Never click links in emails claiming to help you unlock your account.
  2. Choose "Try another way": If the screen prompts for an authenticator code or phone prompt you cannot access, look at the bottom of the box and click Try another way.
  3. Enter an emergency backup code: Select the option to enter one of your 8-digit printed emergency backup codes.
  4. Use your verified recovery email: Choose to have a security verification link delivered to your secondary recovery email address.
  5. Beware of recovery scams: Never pay anyone on Instagram, Discord, or Telegram claiming to be an "ethical hacker" who can recover accounts for a fee. They are scammers taking advantage of your panic.
Someone contacts you asking for… What you must do Why it is dangerous
A temporary 6-digit rolling code Never share it. Hang up immediately. It allows the attacker to authorize their current login attempt right now.
An 8-digit emergency backup code Never share it. Keep it strictly private. It completely bypasses your two-factor authentication and hands over your vault.
Your password manager master password Never share it under any circumstance. It unlocks every single password, debit card, and secure note you have ever saved.

Your recovery readiness checklist

Today: Check that your main email has an up-to-date secondary recovery email.
Today: Verify that your registered recovery phone number is your current active SIM.
Today: Print or write down 10 emergency backup codes and store them in a drawer.
Before changing phones: Verify that Google Authenticator cloud sync is active.
Every 6 months: Review active sessions and remove old, unrecognized computers.
Always: Treat your backup recovery codes like physical keys to your front door.
