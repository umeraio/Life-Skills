What are cookies? (Explained simply)

Imagine visiting a coat-check room. You hand over your coat, and the attendant gives you a small numbered ticket. When you return later, you show that ticket, and they hand your exact coat right back. An internet cookie is that small ticket[cite: 9]!

Important Fact: Cookies are not computer viruses, malware, or programs[cite: 9]. They are tiny text files stored by your browser (such as Chrome, Safari, or Edge) to help websites remember useful preferences, shopping items, and logins[cite: 9].

Helpful cookies vs tracking cookies

Not all cookies do the same job. Here is how to understand the difference between cookies that assist your session and cookies that track your habits[cite: 9]:

First-Party Cookies (Helpful)

Created by: The actual website you are visiting (like YouTube or Amazon)[cite: 9].

What they do: Keep you signed into your profile, remember your preferred language or dark mode theme, and keep items inside your basket as you browse different pages[cite: 9].

Third-Party Cookies (Tracking)

Created by: Outside advertising agencies and tracking networks[cite: 9].

What they do: Follow you from one site to another to see what shoes, games, or tech gadgets you look at, using your browsing history to show targeted adverts across the web[cite: 9].

1. ANDROID / GOOGLE CHROME

How to Clear Cookies on Android Phones

Follow these exact steps inside the Google Chrome app on your Android device[cite: 9]:

Chrome → 3 Dots Menu (⋮) → Settings → Privacy and security → Delete browsing data[cite: 9]
  1. Open Google Chrome: Tap the round Chrome icon on your phone home screen[cite: 9].
  2. Tap the three-dots menu: Look in the top-right corner of the screen and tap the three vertical dots (⋮)[cite: 9].
  3. Open Settings: Scroll down the menu and tap Settings[cite: 9].
  4. Open Privacy: Tap Privacy and security, then tap Delete browsing data[cite: 9].
  5. Select the time range: Tap the dropdown menu next to Time range and choose All time (or Last 7 days if you only want recent data cleared)[cite: 9].
  6. Select what to delete:
    • Check the box for Cookies and site data[cite: 9].
    • Check the box for Cached images and files (this frees up device storage)[cite: 9].
    • CRITICAL: Leave "Saved passwords" and "Autofill data" UNCHECKED so you do not accidentally wipe your saved credentials!
  7. Confirm cleanup: Tap the blue Delete data button in the bottom corner[cite: 9].
✓ Done: Unwanted tracking cookies and temporary image caches have been cleared from Chrome[cite: 9].
2. APPLE IPHONE / IPAD (SAFARI)

How to Clear Cookies on iPhone or iPad

Apple manages Safari browser storage directly through your main iPhone Settings app[cite: 9]:

iPhone Settings → Apps → Safari → Clear History and Website Data[cite: 9]
  1. Open iPhone Settings: Tap the grey gear icon for Settings on your Home Screen[cite: 9].
  2. Find Safari settings: Scroll down and tap on Apps > Safari (on iOS 17 or older, simply scroll down to Safari)[cite: 9].
  3. Locate the clear button: Scroll down the Safari settings page and tap Clear History and Website Data[cite: 9].
  4. Choose your timeframe: Under Clear Timeframe, select All history (or Today and yesterday). Leave Close All Tabs toggled on to start completely fresh.
  5. Confirm the reset: Tap the red button labeled Clear History[cite: 9].
  6. Alternative (Clear specific sites only): If you do not want to be signed out of everything, scroll to the bottom of Safari settings, tap Advanced > Website Data, swipe left on individual websites you do not trust, and tap Delete[cite: 9].
✓ Done: Safari website tracking cookies, caches, and visited links have been removed[cite: 9].
3. WINDOWS PC (CHROME & EDGE)

How to Clear Cookies on Windows PC

Follow these instructions for Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge on your computer[cite: 9]:

Browser → Press Ctrl + Shift + Delete → Select Timeframe → Clear Data[cite: 9]
  1. Use the fast keyboard shortcut: Open your browser (Chrome or Edge) and press Ctrl + Shift + Delete together on your keyboard. This opens the cleanup dashboard instantly!
  2. Or use the menu: Click the three dots (⋮ or …) in the top-right corner > click Settings > Privacy, search, and services (or Privacy and security) > click Clear browsing data[cite: 9].
  3. Set Time range: Click the dropdown menu and pick All time[cite: 9].
  4. Select data boxes carefully:
    • Check Cookies and other site data[cite: 9].
    • Check Cached images and files.
    • CRITICAL: Ensure "Passwords and other sign-in data" is UNCHECKED!
  5. Finalize cleanup: Click the blue button that says Clear data (or Clear now)[cite: 9].
✓ Done: Tracking tokens are erased from your Windows computer browser[cite: 9].
4. MAC (SAFARI & CHROME)

How to Clear Cookies on Mac (macOS)

Follow these steps inside Apple Safari on your Mac desktop or MacBook[cite: 9]:

Safari Menu Bar → Settings → Privacy → Manage Website Data → Remove All[cite: 9]
  1. Open Safari: Launch Safari from your Dock or Launchpad[cite: 9].
  2. Open Safari Settings: Click the word Safari in the top-left Apple menu bar next to the Apple logo, then click Settings (or Preferences)[cite: 9].
  3. Go to Privacy: Click the Privacy tab at the top of the settings window[cite: 9].
  4. Manage site data: Click the button that says Manage Website Data…[cite: 9]
  5. Remove cookies: A list of all websites storing data will appear[cite: 9]. Click Remove All to clear everything, or select specific advertising companies and click Remove[cite: 9].
  6. Confirm: Click Remove Now, then click Done[cite: 9].
✓ Done: Your Mac browser is clean and free of leftover tracking files[cite: 9].

The truth about Incognito / Private Browsing

Many people believe opening an Incognito or Private Browsing window makes them a ghost who cannot be seen by anyone. That is a myth[cite: 9]! Here is what private mode actually does:

What Private Mode DOES do

1. Automatically deletes all cookies, browsing history, and temporary files the exact moment you close the private window[cite: 9].

2. Prevents family members or friends who use the same computer from seeing what websites you visited[cite: 9].

3. Allows you to sign into two different email accounts at the exact same time without logging out of the first one.

What Private Mode DOES NOT do

1. It does not hide your internet activity from your school network, employer, or internet service provider (BT, Virgin, Sky)[cite: 9].

2. It does not stop websites from knowing your approximate city or location.

3. It does not protect you if you download infected files or enter passwords into fake scam links.

Double Check Before Deleting: Never wipe your passwords! When clearing browsing history, always look closely at the checkboxes[cite: 9]. If you tick "Saved Passwords", your browser will delete all your memorized logins[cite: 9]! Use a dedicated password manager (like Bitwarden, Google Password Manager, or Apple Keychain) so your credentials remain protected even after a browser cleanup[cite: 9].

Browser cleanup checklist

  • Clear cookies once a month to remove accumulated tracking networks[cite: 9].
  • Never clear "Saved passwords" or "Autofill form data" during routine cleanups[cite: 9].
  • Be prepared to sign back into common accounts with your two-factor authenticator app after clearing[cite: 9].
  • Use Private / Incognito browsing when booking flights, shopping for surprise presents, or using shared computers[cite: 9].
  • Turn on "Block Third-Party Cookies" inside your browser settings to stop advertising trackers automatically.
